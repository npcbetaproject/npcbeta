Session

Osselin Gloamtoast

Elf · Toastmaster · A toast that sells tomorrow’s triumph

Description

Osselin hosts celebrations in tavern private rooms, drawing shy guests into warm speeches about what they hope to achieve. His ceremonial cup steals one future triumph named in a toast, which he stores in engraved silver leaves and sells to wealthy patrons.

At the table

Welcome each guest warmly, recall something they are proud of and invite them to speak at their own pace. Say, “Your work deserves an audience, my friend. Tell us the success you hope to celebrate next.” His initial friendliness conceals a deliberate invitation into the cup’s bargain.

Personality

Initially friendly, attentive and encouraging; privately envious of people whose work earns applause. He insists that recognition should go to those who can make proper use of it, while craving praise he has never earned.

Motivations

Build a circle of influential clients by supplying stolen victories, then secure a permanent ceremonial office through their patronage. After years of being overlooked as a performer, he treats other people’s promise as something to trade rather than respect.

Adventure hook

A craftworker who toasted an upcoming exhibition watches a wealthy stranger win with her own distinctive design and asks the party to attend Osselin’s next taproom celebration. GM secret: the cup transfers a named future triumph into a silver leaf hidden in its stem; Osselin sells the leaf and keeps a commission ledger in a rented storeroom. Two well-paid ushers screen his private gatherings, and indebted patrons discourage questions. The party can compare the ledger with guests’ failed ambitions, approach a dissatisfied buyer or secure the cup before another toast. Its binding has an exploitable contradiction: if the buyer publicly credits the person whose triumph was taken, that leaf releases its claim. Osselin needs the stolen success to look deserved, so persuading one patron to acknowledge the source can unravel his reputation. Recovering the cup and unused leaves prevents further theft; returning the leaves restores their owners’ chance to achieve the promised successes through their own efforts, without guaranteeing an outcome.
